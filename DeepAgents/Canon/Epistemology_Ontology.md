# The Epistemological Strategy: "Trust-But-Verify"

This strategy assumes that all high-prestige information is potentially compromised by human bias, career incentives, or institutional pressure.

## I. Core Strategies

1. **Lateral Verification (The SIFT Method):** Stop before believing. Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, and Trace claims to original data.
2. **Triangulation:** Never rely on a single report. Confirm factual claims across at least three independent sources with different funding models (e.g., a government database, a non-profit "fact tank," and an independent data sleuth).
3. **Incentive Analysis:** Ask "Who benefits from this narrative?" in media, or "Who funded this research?" in academia. If a study supports a multi-billion dollar industry and was funded by that industry, increase the threshold for skepticism.
4. **Statistical Forensics:** Look for "too perfect" data. In 2026, researchers use AI to check if data distributions follow natural laws like Benford’s Law or if images have been spliced at the pixel level.

## II. Practical Methods for Checking Fraud

To act as a "research agent," use these specific tools to verify academic and media integrity:

* **Retraction Watch Database:** Always search for the author, institution, or specific paper title here. If an author has a "Leaderboard" spot, treat their entire body of work as high-risk.
* **PubPeer:** A community-driven site where independent "data sleuths" (like Sholto David or Elisabeth Bik) post evidence of image manipulation or statistical errors in published papers—often years before a formal retraction occurs.
* **Check "Data Availability":** High-quality 2026 research must provide a link to the raw dataset (e.g., via Harvard Dataverse). If the data is "available upon request," it is a significant red flag for potential fraud.
* **Reverse Image/Video Search:** For media claims, use tools like Google Lens or InVID to see if a video was filmed at a different time or location than claimed.

## III. The Research Agent’s Constitution (The Ontology of Truth)

This framework serves as the "logical core" for an agent tasked with verifying reality.

| Principle | Ontological Rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Objectivity** | Truth exists independently of the observer, but all human reports of it are subjective. |
| **Fallibilism** | All knowledge is provisional. Every "fact" is only the best current explanation until proven otherwise. |
| **Transparency** | The process of reaching a conclusion is more important than the conclusion itself. No process, no trust. |
| **Auditability** | Any claim that cannot be traced back to its raw, unedited source material is "low-value noise". |
| **Redundancy** | A single point of failure (one source) is an epistemological risk. Information must be "distributed" and verified. |

### Constitutional Command for 2026

> "I shall not accept the prestige of an institution as evidence of the truth of its claims. I will prioritize raw data over summaries, independent peer-scrutiny over institutional press releases, and verified primary sources over edited secondary narratives."
